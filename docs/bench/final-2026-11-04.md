# Pre-release benchmark, 2026-11-04

The query stream is private and not in this repository: 12,692 real names (distinct name and qtype), about 29% of them blocked by the lists. The tables hold aggregates only, so you cannot reproduce these numbers exactly. You can repeat the method with any name list; see [bench/competitors/README.md](../../bench/competitors/README.md).

## Summary

- Saturation over UDP: 94.1k qps with a plain upstream, 94.6k with DoH (medians of three runs). Over TCP: 54.5k and 52.4k (one run each).
- Hit latency p99: 1.9 ms at 50 qps and 0.91 ms at 500 qps (plain), 2.7 and 1.7 ms (DoH). No queries lost at 5,000 qps.
- Resident memory: 18.9-19.5 MiB with the four lists loaded (744,512 entries, 516,214 unique rules). It does not grow under load.
- Cache hits inside the process: p50 at most 4 us, p99 at most 8 us, at every offered rate from 200 qps to closed loop. The round trip (0.5-0.66 ms p50 on the LAN) is network and OS time.
- AdGuard Home stops answering in the first UDP saturation phase on both transports (6.7k then 0 qps plain, 3.4k then 0 DoH), with its cgroup at the 512 MiB limit. Its saturation and TCP cells record a dead server.

- CPU: sopedu-dns used 88-90% of its one core in every saturation run (server Pod CPU cgroup). CFS never throttles a single-threaded process on a 1 CPU quota. dnsperf used at most 110% of a core against its 2 CPU limit, so the server is the limit. Throttled periods: Blocky 84-98%, Pi-hole 18-76%, AdGuard Home 52% in its one live run.
- Saturation on this node is bimodal for the fast servers: for sopedu-dns and Unbound alike, one run in three sits near 64k instead of 94-107k. Read the medians with the ranges.

We state an ordering only when the three-run ranges do not overlap and a bootstrap 95% confidence interval of the median ratio excludes 1.0.

- sopedu-dns against Blocky, Pi-hole and AdGuard Home: the ranges are far apart in every column (5.7x Blocky, 23x Pi-hole on the whole stream; AdGuard Home is dead). TCP against Blocky is 17x (plain) and 21x (DoH).
- sopedu-dns against Unbound: no ordering. On the whole stream 64.2-106.7k overlaps 49.9-77.4k, and the unblocked and pure-hit ranges overlap too. TCP is one run each (54.5k and 52.4k against 57.8k), too few to rank.
- sopedu-dns against dnsmasq: on the whole and unblocked streams dnsmasq forwards the HTTPS names (24% of the stream) to the upstream because it cannot cache them, so the gap comes from the workload. On pure hits the three-run ranges nearly separate (72.3k against 77.2k). No ordering.

## Fairness notes

- dnsmasq and Pi-hole (FTL is based on dnsmasq) do not cache HTTPS records, which are 24% of the stream, and forward those names on every query. This explains most of their hit latency and their whole-stream saturation.
- dnsmasq caches at most 10k names.
- Pi-hole ran with its defaults, which log every query (`dns.queryLogging = true`, 91-day database), and was throttled in 18-76% of CFS periods. In a separate run with query logging and the database off it was about 3x faster on pure cache hits, but FTL aborted after about 1M queries when the Pod's 64 MiB `/dev/shm` filled up, so these tables keep the defaults.
- Unbound and dnsmasq run without lists and block nothing. Their throughput and memory are for a lighter workload.

## Cache hits inside the process

### Method

- `examples/hit_rtt.rs` sends one query at a time over 1,000 warm names and times 100,000 hits with exact percentiles.
- It ran from `node-b` against the idle sopedu-dns Pod on `node-a`: same image and config as the matrix, plain mode, lists loaded, `ui_listen = 127.0.0.1:19998`.
- Server-side figure: the difference in the `dns_request_duration_seconds` histogram from `/metrics` before and after each run. It covers only work inside the process, from `recv` returning to the reply being built. The buckets are log2, so the figures are upper bounds.
- `dns_queries_total{result="upstream"}` did not change during any timed run, so every timed query was a cache hit. The Pod was primed first, so the 1,000 warm-up queries of each run (1% of the 101,000 server-side samples) are hits too.

| Gap | Offered rate | Client RTT p50 / p99 / p99.9 (us), node-b to node-a over the LAN | Server-side p50 / p99 / p99.9 (us, upper bounds) | Loopback on node-a: RTT p50 / p99 / p99.9 (us) |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | closed loop (about 1.8k qps over the LAN) | 556 / 872 / 3,086 | at most 2 / 8 / 64 | not run |
| 20 us | | 508 / 827 / 2,395 | at most 2 / 8 / 64 | not run |
| 100 us | about 10k qps | 636 / 724 / 1,177 | at most 2 / 4 / 32 | 30.0 / 88.0 / 222 |
| 500 us | about 2k qps | 617 / 715 / 1,292 (second run: 635 / 717 / 1,184) | at most 2 / 4 / 32 | 75.7 / 122.7 / 739 |
| 5 ms | about 200 qps | 659 / 897 / 4,551 (20,000 hits) | at most 4 / 8 / 32 | 78.8 / 168.3 / 279 |

### Result

- Inside the process a cache hit takes at most 4 us at p50 and at most 8 us at p99 at all five offered rates. p99.9 is at most 64 us.
- The round trip is larger and includes network and OS time. Over the LAN: 0.5-0.66 ms at p50 (network time, matching the 0.6-0.7 ms p50 of every server in the matrix) and 0.7-0.9 ms at p99. On loopback (client on the same node): 30-79 us at p50, 88-168 us at p99.
- The share of loopback round trips over 100 us grows as the offered rate falls: 0.6%, 4.8% and 11% at 100 us, 500 us and 5 ms gaps. That is thread wake-up and cold CPU caches at low rates, which the process cannot shorten.
